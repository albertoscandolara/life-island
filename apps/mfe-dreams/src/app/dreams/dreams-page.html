1. Write it as you would tell a friend: why it matters, where you are today, what scares you, what you cannot put at risk.
  2. Answer a few questions. Ovi asks what it still needs to know, including the one thing inside you most likely to stop you.
  3. Get a path. With the first steps comes a path of up to five milestones: clear on what you want, know what is out there, ready to try, tried it for real, decided. Each step names the milestone it serves, so you always know what it is for. Change the milestones as you like; tell Ovi when you are there.
  4. Pick one step and plan it: a day, a moment, and what you do if you start putting it off.
  5. Check in after it: how it felt, how hard it really was, what it showed you. The next steps follow.

Overview shows the one thing that moves each dream now. Ovi suggests; you decide, always. Nothing happens until you pick a step.

Psychologists call it graded exposure: you face what you avoid in steps small enough to take, and each one makes the next feel smaller. That is why every step says how far out of the comfort zone it goes (tiny, small, brave), what it costs, and how to undo it.

  • Your reach starts from the pace you chose: gentle (tiny steps), steady (up to small) or bolder (up to brave).
  • Two good steps in a row stretch you a little further, without asking: that is how confidence grows. Ask for smaller at any check-in and it always wins.
  • Two heavy check-ins in a row make the next round tiny and short.
  • Doing beats thinking. After two reflection steps in a row, the next ones take you outside: a message, a call, a visit, a try. Motivation usually comes after the action, not before.
  • Too big? Take the smaller one. Every step has a smaller version; a small step taken beats a big one waiting.

These are implementation intentions, one of the best studied tools in psychology. When you plan a step you write:

  • When: the day, which goes in the Agenda.
  • The moment: a cue you already live, “after breakfast on Saturday”, “on the train home”.
  • If it gets hard: “If I notice I am putting it off, then I do only the smaller version”. Deciding in advance what you do at the hard moment means you do not have to be brave then.

Ovi's questions also follow WOOP: the wish, the best outcome, the inner obstacle (a fear, a habit, a thought), and the plan for it. A chosen step with no day shows in Needs you until it has one.

When you plan a step, say how hard you expect it to be; when you check in, how hard it really was. Most steps turn out easier than feared, and seeing it in your own numbers (Overview, “What you feared, and how it went”) weakens the next fear. Psychologists call these behavioural experiments.

After each step, write one line on what it showed you: “I can ask a stranger a question”. They collect in Proof you can. Confidence (self-efficacy) is built from things you did, not from things you were told: read them again on the days you doubt yourself.

  • Be kind to yourself. A skipped step is information, not a verdict: it comes back smaller.
  • Resting is allowed. Rest a dream from its page; nothing is lost and no date is late. After two weeks without a step, Ovi only suggests a smaller one, or a rest.
  • Letting go is allowed too. A dream that no longer fits can make room for one that does.

Life Island helps you plan small steps; it is not a coach or a therapist. If things feel heavy for a while, talking to someone you trust, or to a professional, can really help.

Questions reads the dream as you wrote it and asks what it still needs to know. Nothing from the other areas is sent.

Small steps reads the dream, your answers, the path, the steps you took with how hard you expected and found them, your check-ins, and only the areas you tick in Safety net (the facts shown there, never names of companies, people or places).

Without a Claude API key, or with a tool off, the questions come from Life Island's own list for each area of life, and the steps from its own ladder (think, explore, prepare, try, decide), each with its cue and its if-then.

Tick a habit on the days you do it and give it a number of times a week: the tile shows how many are on target. Your week asks what went well, what got in the way and what you will focus on: a short look back is what keeps a dream from drifting.
